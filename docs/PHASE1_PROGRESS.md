### Follow-up to slice 3: the 100 ms save, explained and fixed
**Cause:** SQLite's FTS5 merges index segments as part of a commit. On the 100,000-row ledger with the trigram index, that merge landed on save number 10 or 11 (`#10 = 53 ms` in a default-settings run). Experiments on fresh 100k-row databases (Android 8 emulator, SQLCipher): default `#10 = 53 ms`; `wal_autocheckpoint=0` did not change it (so not the WAL checkpoint); `automerge=0` alone removed the second spike but not the first; **`automerge=0` plus a high `crisismerge` made every save 1 ms or less.** A checkpoint after bulk loads, tried earlier, did nothing and was removed.

**Fix (`FtsTuning`):** merging is switched off at open (idempotent, and re-applying writes nothing; settings are stored in the database) with `crisismerge=1000` as a safety net, and the app merges the index in small steps when the screen goes to the background (`AppContainer.maintain`, at most about 70 ms per step while holding the write lock, 2 s budget).
- **Saves:** p50 0.36 ms, **p95 0.92 ms, slowest of 60 saves 6.3 ms** (was p95 5-8 ms and slowest ~100 ms). With 1,000 unmerged saves, p95 0.58 ms and the slowest 5 ms.
- **Cost:** searches read more segments until the idle merge runs: with 1,000 unmerged segments a common search went from 8.2 to 9.8 ms and a rare one from 12.2 to 15.3 ms. Merging 1,000 segments took about 0.76 s in total, in steps of at most 67 ms.
- **Tests:** search answers are identical before and after merging 600 separate commits including soft-deletes and edits (host, and 300 commits on the device through the app's container); merging reports "done" once finished; the tuning persists and is in place after the app opens the database. The gate now also limits the **slowest** save (100 ms phone budget, 25 ms here) and I confirmed by mutation that removing the tuning makes it fail (107.7 ms).
- **The safety net, measured:** at about 1,000 unmerged saves SQLite forces a merge: save #994 took 52 ms and #998 26 ms (every other save stayed at or below 5 ms; p95 0.6-0.7 ms over 1,300-2,500 saves). A lower threshold did **not** shrink the stall and made it more frequent: 100 ms at 250, 59 ms at 100, 61 ms at 50, with 9 to 11 saves over 5 ms instead of 3. So the threshold stays at 1,000, and the answer is to keep the pile from reaching it. Merging the whole pile in idle time took 0.69 s (17 steps); searches after 2,500 unmerged saves were 9.3 ms ('upi') and 13.8 ms ('order 77') against 8.2 and 12.6 ms merged.
- **Widget-only users:** the widget now spends up to 300 ms merging the index after each tap, once the confirmation is on screen, so the pile stays near zero even if the app is never opened. A device test makes 6 widget taps and asserts nothing is left to merge; removing the merge makes it fail. The merge runs only when the tap succeeded.
- **Not yet done:** the interval between idle merges is not tuned (they run whenever the activity stops); the tile and shortcut open the app, whose stop-time merge covers them; a user who records only through the SMS receiver (Phase 2) will need the same call there.

**Reading the earlier "p95":** with 60 samples the 95th percentile is the third-slowest save, and the first save after opening (about 5-6 ms, warm-up) is usually one of them, so the old p95 of 5-8 ms mostly reflected warm-up, and the gate's earlier 3.8-4.3 ms vs 7-9 ms difference was not a regression in the save path (the same insert, on a database that had become slightly different by then). The slowest-save check is the more informative number.

**Write-ahead-log housekeeping (answers to review points):** a test shows `journal_mode` is still `wal` after closing and reopening, and that `EncryptedDriver.reset` leaves no `-wal` or `-shm` file behind. The backup and device-transfer rules exclude the whole database domain, which covers those files. **When the export feature is built, it must checkpoint (`PRAGMA wal_checkpoint(TRUNCATE)`) before copying the database file, because committed changes can still sit in the `-wal` file.** The gate's save test soft-deletes its 60 rows in a `finally`; the dataset check counts only live rows, so that is sufficient, but those rows and their search entries remain in the throwaway test database.

**Totals:** 136 host/JVM tests (both targets counted), 43 device tests on Android 8.0 (1 skipped: widget binding, Android 10+); full chain from deleted result files, build green.

## Slice 5: budgets (FR-BUD-01 modes a and b, FR-BUD-02, FR-BUD-04, safe-to-spend from FR-BUD-05)
**Built:**
- **One overall limit and any number of category limits.** A category limit counts that category and its subcategories (a "Food" limit includes "Food › Cafe"); the overall limit counts everything including uncategorised. Spending is your net share: refunds are taken off and only your own part of a shared expense counts.
- **Periods:** calendar month, a monthly cycle that starts on any day 1-28 (for a salary on the 25th), or weekly from a chosen weekday. Periods tile the calendar with no gap or overlap; the 29th-31st are not allowed as start days because some months lack them. The list heading says "this month", "this cycle" or "this week" to match.
- **List:** under the spending total, a bar, "₹3,000.00 left of ₹20,000.00", and "about ₹115.38 a day". Over the limit it says "₹100.00 over of ₹800.00" in the error colour and the per-day figure goes away. The bar changes colour at 80% and when over; the text says the same, so colour is never the only signal. A line notes how many category budgets are near or over. Tapping opens the **Budget screen**: period, the overall limit, and every category with its limit, bar and remaining amount. Setting or editing a limit is a short dialog (the current value is pre-selected, so typing replaces it); "Remove limit" is in the same dialog.
- **Safe to spend** = what is left under the overall limit, divided by the days left in the period including today, rounded **down** so it never promises more than is left. On the last day the whole remainder is available; once the limit is reached it is zero. **It does not subtract upcoming bills or planned savings, because recurring items do not exist yet;** the Budget screen says so in words.
- **Interaction with categories:** merging moves the source's limit to the target unless the target has its own (then the target's wins); deleting a category removes its limit; setting a limit with a merged id lands on the target. Limits and the period are written to the sync log; the change `merged_into`-style re-mapping for limits is applied by the merge itself, so a future sync must replay it (same contract as entries).

**Verified:**
- 15 domain tests on both targets: property tests that periods tile the calendar for every start day, leap years and February, a cycle across a year boundary, exact 80%/at-limit/over boundaries, refunds making spending negative, overflow, safe-to-spend rounding never exceeding the remainder, time zone handling. 14 store tests: the period edges to the minute (23:59 on the last day is in, midnight is out), subcategories, shared expenses and refunds (including a refund in a later period reducing that period, not the original), the 25th-cycle boundary, persistence, no log entry when nothing changed, other-currency limits kept but not shown, and the merge/delete interactions. 7 device tests on Android 8 driving the real screens.
- **Mutation checks:** 5 on the domain math (over-at-limit, the 80% edge, today not counted as a day left, subcategories ignored, the start day landing in the wrong period) and 8 on the store (period start or end off by a minute, subcategories ignored, no redirect for a merged id, currency filter removed, merge overwriting the target's limit, merge leaving a limit behind, delete leaving one behind) plus 1 on the UI (the pre-selected text): all caught.
- **Looked at on the emulator:** the list with the bar and per-day figure (26 days left on 6 Oct, 3000.00 / 26 = 115.38, checked by hand), the Budget screen with an over-limit category, and the limit dialog with the keyboard confirmed up (`mInputShown=true`): Save at y=1037, above the keyboard.

**Two bugs the tests and screens caught while building it:** (1) editing an existing limit put the cursor at the start of the field, so typing "800" over "1000.00" produced "8001000.00", a valid but wrong amount; fixed by selecting the current value, with a test that fails without the fix. (2) The summary row is one merged node for screen readers (which is right), so my first tests looked in the wrong place; the tests now look at the merged text where that is what a user hears.

**Not done / caveats:**
- **No alerts yet** (FR-BUD-04's notifications at 80% and 100%): the near/over state is shown on screen but nothing notifies you. That waits for the notification work.
- **Rollover, envelope and zero-based modes, per-category-per-month overrides, and budgets for tags** are not built; the limit is the same every period.
- **Safe to spend ignores bills and income.** Treat it as "what is left of the cap, spread evenly", not a forecast.
- **Single currency in the UI:** limits in other currencies are stored but only the primary currency (INR) is shown.
- **Amber (80%+) was not checked for contrast** in dark theme or by TalkBack; large-font layout unchecked. Android 8 emulator only.
- **Deleting a category refuses while used, but a limit alone does not block deletion** (it is removed with it), by design.
- The period is global, not per budget.

## Slice 6: repeating expenses (FR-REC-01, and the bills part of safe-to-spend)
**Built:**
- **Schedules:** every N days, weeks, months or years from a start date, ending never, on a date, or after a number of times. Each date is worked out from the start and its position, never from the previous date, so a monthly item that starts on the 31st falls on 31 Jan, 28 Feb (29 in a leap year), 31 Mar, 30 Apr and back to 31 May without drifting; a yearly 29 Feb falls on 28 Feb in other years.
- **Due items on the list:** under the budget summary, each waiting occurrence shows "Rent · ₹15,000.00 · Due today" (or "Due 1 Oct · 5 days ago", in the error colour) with **Skip** and **Post**. Up to three show; more says "N more waiting" and opens the **Repeating** screen (button in the list header), which lists waiting items and every repeating expense, with a full-screen editor (name, amount, category, account, repeat interval and unit, start, end, "post by itself") with Save pinned above the keyboard.
- **Post** creates a normal expense dated on the due day, with a fixed id per occurrence, so the same occurrence can never be posted twice (a second try does nothing). **Skip** records that it was dealt with without spending. Both show an **Undo** snackbar; undoing a post deletes the expense and makes the occurrence due again, and posting it again restores the same entry instead of failing.
- **No fixed amount** (electricity): leave the amount empty; Post asks for it in a short dialog. Such an item cannot be set to post by itself.
- **Post by itself:** items with a fixed amount and that switch on are posted when the app comes to the front, with a snackbar ("Posted Netflix"), and can be undone like any other. Everything else waits for your Post or Skip.
- **Edits affect future occurrences only.** Posted expenses are ordinary entries and stay as they are; an occurrence already posted or skipped is remembered by its date, so changing the amount, the schedule or even the start date never re-posts or forgets one.
- **Window:** only the last 60 days are offered. An occurrence nobody dealt with for longer than that is no longer shown; it is not auto-posted either. (60 is my choice, not tested with users.)
- **Safe to spend now sets bills aside:** the repeating expenses still waiting in the current budget period (including ones overdue and unposted, which will post inside it) are subtracted before spreading what is left over the days left, e.g. "After ₹3,000.00 of repeating bills still to come, you can spend about ₹X a day". If the bills use up everything left, it says "bills use it all". Bills in the next period, already posted, skipped, or without a fixed amount are not counted (the text says the last).
- **Categories:** a repeating expense counts as a use of its category (so it blocks deleting the category), follows merges, and a late edit with a merged id is redirected. Changes are written to the sync log field by field; posts and skips are logged as per-occurrence entries.

**Verified:** 8 schedule tests on both targets (including a property test that the fast skip-ahead agrees with walking from the start for every unit), 13 store tests, 2 more safe-to-spend maths tests and 1 store test for bills, 9 device tests on Android 8 driving the real screens (create, post, skip+undo, undo of a post, ask for an amount, auto-post on open and not twice, bills set aside, "bills use it all", edit rules, delete keeping posted entries). **Mutation checks:** 4 on the schedule, 8 on the store, 3 on safe-to-spend and 4 on the UI behaviour; two survived at first and were real gaps in my tests (a past-due unposted bill was never exercised; the undo mutation did not compile), fixed and re-run. The device suite for this slice also passed 3 times in a row. I looked at the editor with the keyboard confirmed up (Save above it) and at the list with a due card.

**One real bug found by a test:** posting an occurrence that was posted, undone and posted again failed ("Entry ... is deleted"), because undo only marks the entry deleted and the re-post reused its id. It now restores the entry first.

**Not done / caveats:**
- **No reminders or notifications** (FR-REC-03 "bill due reminders" needs notifications, which are not built). Due items are only seen when the app is open. A bill's statement balance, minimum due and card due dates are not modelled.
- **Auto-post happens only when the app is opened**, not in the background, so an item can post a day or more late (it is dated on its due day). Background posting needs a scheduled job, which I have not built.
- **No "this vs. future" edit choice** beyond "edits apply to occurrences not yet posted"; no editing of a single occurrence's amount except when it has none; no recurring income or transfers (FR-REC-01 lists them) and no detection of recurring patterns (FR-REC-02), subscriptions, EMIs or IOUs.
- **Safe to spend still ignores income and planned savings.**
- **The three header buttons are cramped** on a narrow screen; large fonts, dark theme and TalkBack are unchecked. Android 8 emulator only.
- **Time zones:** a due day is a calendar date; travelling across time zones could shift when "today" begins. Not tested.

**Totals:** 243 host/JVM tests (both targets counted), 68 device tests (1 skipped: widget binding, Android 10+); full chain from deleted result files, gradle exit 0.

## Slice 8: export and backup (FR-DATA-04 partly, FR-DATA-05 partly)
**Built** (button "Data" in the list header):

## Fix after slice 11: a startup race that could leave the app on its opening screen forever
**What was wrong.** The view model starts loading the list on a background thread inside its `init` block, and that block was declared *above* a property the load needs (the date formatter used to draw each row). If the background thread got to a row before the constructor had created the formatter, it hit a null and the load died with a `NullPointerException` that nothing reported, so **the opening spinner never went away**. This has been in the code since the list screen was first written. On this emulator it happened on about 3% of starts of an isolated test (and more often in the full suite), so on a real phone an occasional cold start could have hung. It surfaced as an intermittent failure of "an item that posts by itself is posted when the app opens", which failed 2 of 5 runs, then 1 of 10, then once in 30.

**How it was found** (recorded because I guessed wrong first): I first blamed a stale refresh racing an automatic post, and made the initial load take the same write lock as every other refresh (a sound change on its own, kept). It still failed 1 in 10, so I stopped guessing, added logging, looped the one test until it failed, then caught and logged the exception: the log showed the load starting and never publishing, and the stack trace showed the null formatter.

**Fix:** the `init` block is now the last thing in the class, after every property. **Regression test:** builds and drops the view model 400 times and requires every load to finish; it passes with the fix (two runs) and fails with the old order (two of two). It also relates to the unexplained failures logged earlier (the Compose crash on destroy is probably unrelated, but I cannot rule it out).

**New verification procedure (`tools/verify.sh`)**, adopted after a review point: step 1 runs the build, host tests, release build and every device test except the timing gate; step 2 runs the timing gate alone after waiting for the host's load average to fall (it waited 45 s this time, load 12). Both must pass. The gates are unchanged; they simply no longer run while Gradle and the emulator compete for the machine. This run: 348 host/JVM tests (both targets counted), 104 functional device tests (1 skipped) and 11 timing-gate tests; verified in two steps by tools/verify.sh from deleted result files.

**Correction to the app-lock notes, now closed:** I had said cancelling the real system prompt leaves the app locked, but my UI dumps for that step came back empty, so it was inferred (the fake-prompt tests cover it). I redid it on the emulator with a real PIN: after 68 s away the real prompt appeared; pressing Back showed the app's own screen reading "Locked / Your expenses are hidden until you unlock / Unlock"; tapping Unlock reopened the real prompt; the PIN returned to the same screen. If the layout is wrong you change the account, how many rows to skip, each column, the date order, or whether plus or minus means spending (credit cards usually show purchases as positive), and the preview updates at once. If the dates could be day-first or month-first it says so.
- **What it reads:** comma, semicolon, tab or pipe separated files; quoted cells with commas, quotes and line breaks; dates such as 07/10/2026, 07-10-26, 7 Oct 2026, 2026-10-07 (a time of day after the date is ignored); amounts such as 1,23,456.78 (Indian grouping) or 1,234,567.89, with ₹, Rs. or INR, minus signs, brackets, a trailing minus, or Dr/Cr; files in UTF-8, UTF-16 or old Windows-1252 encoding. **A cell it cannot read safely is reported, never guessed**: an amount like 12,50 or 1.234,56 is refused rather than read as the wrong number, and impossible dates (31/02) are refused.
- **Importing the same file twice, or two exports that overlap, adds nothing twice.** Each entry's identity is made from its account, day, direction, amount and description (ignoring case and spacing), plus how many identical rows came before it in the file, so two identical coffees on one day stay two entries, always the same two. Something you deleted is not brought back and something you edited is not overwritten. Everything saves in one step (nothing if anything fails), oldest first. **It does not recognise a transaction you typed in by hand**, so an expense you logged yourself and then imported will appear twice.
- Imported entries are left **uncategorised**, with the bank's description as the merchant (so Search finds them). 5,000 rows imported in 1.5 s on the desktop test machine; not timed on a phone.
- **Also fixed on the way:** the list now shows income and refunds with a plus sign and the accent colour. Before, a salary looked exactly like a purchase (the "Spent this month" total was right; only the rows were unclear). The unused leftover `outflow` field was replaced by `inflow`.

**Verified:** 9 CSV-reader tests (including a round trip of cells made from commas, quotes, line breaks and tabs, and remembering the file line each row started on), 24 statement tests (dates in every common format, two-digit-year pivot, plausible-year range, ambiguity detection; amounts in either grouping, brackets, Dr/Cr, a property test that any amount written in Indian or western grouping reads back exactly, and 15 spellings that must be refused; six layouts modelled on common bank exports), 13 importer tests (idempotency, overlap, identical rows, deleted and edited entries, order, wrong account or currency, failure in the middle, 5,000 rows), 9 device tests on Android 8 driving the real screen with files. **Mutation checks:** 8 on the CSV reader, 21 on the statement logic, 12 on the importer, 8 on the screen; several survived at first and each got a specific test (the delimiter chosen by commas inside quotes, a detector accepting a preamble line as a header, the currency check masked by the ledger's own error, an account-selection mutation masked by a test that chose that very account). Three runs of the device tests passed. I imported a sample statement through the **real system file picker** on the emulator and looked at the screens.

**All statement fixtures are synthetic.** I invented layouts modelled on how HDFC, ICICI and SBI-style exports and card statements are commonly shaped; **none is a real statement and none has been checked against a real bank's file**, so a real export may need a manual column choice. No real bank file has been tried at all.
