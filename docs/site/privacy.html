Privacy Policy for Expenses

Effective EFFECTIVE_DATE. Applies to the Expenses app for Android and iPhone ("the app"), published by Chandrashekhar Nelluri ("I", "me").

In one sentence: everything you enter stays on your phone, the app has no way to send it anywhere by itself, and I collect nothing about you unless you choose to email me.

What the app stores

The expenses, incomes, accounts, categories, budgets, goals, bills, trips, tags, notes and settings you enter; the bank or payment alerts you choose to let it read (see below); the photos and files you attach; and a few facts the app keeps for itself, such as when you last made a backup and when the last bank text arrived. All of it is kept on your phone. On Android the database is encrypted with a key protected by the Android Keystore (in the phone's secure hardware where the phone has it); attached photos and files are kept in the app's private storage, which Android encrypts. On iPhone everything is protected by iOS Data Protection.

What I collect and share

Nothing, unless you email me (see "If you email me"). The app has no account, no sign-in, no advertising, no analytics, no crash reporting and no tracking. It is free: no subscriptions and no in-app purchases. The libraries inside the Android app (Google's ML Kit text recognizer, PdfBox, SQLCipher, AndroidX) run on your phone, and the usage reporting ML Kit would add is removed. The Android app does not have the internet permission, so it cannot send data off your phone even by mistake. You can check this in the app: More, Verify it yourself. I do not sell or share any data, because I do not have any.

Things you can choose to switch on

  • Reading bank texts (Android). Only while Read bank texts is on, after you read the explanation the app shows and grant Android's permission. The app checks the sender of each incoming text first and reads a text from a bank, card or biller on your phone to find a transaction or a bill. Texts from people, one-time passwords and unknown senders are dropped without their words being kept; they are only counted, for the "How capture is doing" page. For a sender it skipped, the app keeps only the sender's name (for example AX-HDFCBK), how many texts were skipped and when the last one came, so you can tell the app that this sender is your bank. No words or numbers from those texts are kept. A transaction you do not accept stays in the review list until you delete it.
  • Reading payment-app notifications (Android). Only while this switch is on and you have given Notification access. The app reads only the notifications of Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm and BHIM, to find a payment. Notifications from every other app are ignored without being read.
  • Reading bank alert emails from notifications (Android). A separate switch, off until you turn it on; it also needs Notification access. Only while it is on, the app reads the notifications of Gmail, Outlook and Yahoo Mail (the sender's name, the subject and the start of the message the email app shows). It keeps only an email from a bank or card issuer on its built-in list (or a name you add) whose words read like a payment, a credit or a bill, and puts it in your To review list. Every other email is dropped without being kept. Nothing is sent anywhere.
  • Check a message. In More, Capture, "Why isn't capture working?", you can paste a message (on Android you can also share one to the app from another app's Share sheet) to see how the app would read it. The app reads the clipboard only when you tap Paste. It reads that text on your phone and shows the result on the screen; the text is not saved and not sent anywhere, and it is gone when you leave the page. Nothing is read unless you paste or share it, and this check does not open your messages.
  • Bank messages and emails on iPhone. iPhone does not let any app read messages, emails or other apps' notifications. If you set up a Shortcuts automation, share a text or email to the app from the Share sheet, or paste one, the app reads that text on your phone with the same rules as above. The Share sheet and the widgets keep a small amount of data (the shared words until the app reads them; the figures a widget shows) in storage shared only between the app and its own extensions on your phone.
  • Photos and attachments. You can take a photo of a bill or attach a photo or file to an entry. On Android the app uses your phone's camera app and has no camera permission; on iPhone it asks for camera access. Photos and files stay in the app's private storage on your phone. When you ask the app to read the items of a bill photo or a statement (a photo, PDF or Excel file), it reads it on your phone; on Android with ML Kit, whose model is inside the app and works offline. Nothing is uploaded.
  • Widgets and notifications. A widget you place shows figures on your home screen; Hide amounts masks them. Reminders leave amounts out while Hide amounts or the App lock is on.
  • Reminders and milestones. Bill and budget reminders are made on your phone from your own data. Milestones, off until you turn them on, are cards inside the app, not notifications. Nothing is sent to a server.
  • Travel mode. A trip tags the entries dated within its days. It uses dates only: the app never asks for your location.
  • App lock. The app asks your phone's fingerprint, face or passcode check. The app never sees your fingerprint or face.
  • Files you choose. Export (CSV or Excel), backup, import and automatic copies use files and folders you pick yourself. A backup can be protected with a passphrase you choose; if you forget it the file cannot be opened, by me or anyone. Where you save or send a file (Drive, Mail, AirDrop) is your choice and that service's own policy applies to it.

Backups made by your phone

On Android the app's data is left out of Google's cloud backup and of phone-to-phone transfer. On iPhone your iCloud or computer backup of the phone includes the app's data, as it does for other apps; that backup is under your Apple account and Apple's policy. To move to a new phone, use the app's own backup.

If you email me

Send feedback (in More) and the report of a message the app did not understand each open your own mail app with an email to me filled in. Feedback includes the app version, the system version and the phone model. A message report includes that message with numbers, names, codes and (unless you choose to keep the payment amount) amounts masked, and you see it before it goes. Nothing is sent unless you press Send in your mail app. I use what you send only to answer you and to fix the app, and I delete it if you ask.

Permissions (Android)

Notifications (reminders); fingerprint or face unlock (app lock); start after restart, stay awake briefly and run a short task (reminders and automatic copies); see whether there is a connection (added by a system library; the app still cannot use the internet); and receiving texts (RECEIVE_SMS, only if you turn on Read bank texts and agree on the screen that explains it). Notification access is a setting you turn on yourself. The app does not ask for location, contacts, camera, microphone or your phone's files. None of these permissions sends data anywhere.

Retention and deleting your data

Your data stays on your phone until you delete it. You can delete entries in the app, wipe everything with Start fresh, or uninstall the app, which removes its data. Because I hold no copy, I cannot recover or delete anything for you, except an email you sent me.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and I do not knowingly collect anything from anyone.

Changes

If this policy changes I will update this page and the date above. The app will not start collecting data without a new version and a clear notice.
