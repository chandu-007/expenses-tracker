# Phase 2 progress: India auto-capture

Slices continue the numbering of Phase 1. Same rules: every claim says what was exercised, every number its conditions, and what was not done is stated.

## Slice 34: the capture pipeline (FR-CAP-05, first half; FR-CAP-12 storage)
**Built:** the part that turns a text into a pending item, with no screen yet. **Sender rules** (`SenderRules`): a header like `AX-HDFCBK-S` is reduced to its root `HDFCBK` (operator prefix and category suffix dropped, case ignored); a phone number has no header and is never captured. **Classifier** (`CaptureClassifier`): the sender is judged **before the body is read**, so a text from a person or an unknown header is never parsed; then OTP, notices (due dates, KYC) and declined payments are set aside with their own reason; only a transaction from an allowlisted header reaches the inbox, always with confidence `GENERIC` (the Phase 0 finding that generic parsing misreads about 1 in 65 directions stands: nothing may skip review). **Inbox store**: pending, confirmed and dismissed items; the text of a message is kept only while the item is pending and is erased when it is confirmed or dismissed; the same text delivered twice is one item (fingerprint of source, sender, the time the phone stamped it and the normalised words, so two identical-looking texts at different times are two transactions). **Capture events**: for every text looked at, one row of time, header root and outcome, **never the words or numbers**, pruned to 90 days; this is what capture health will read. The person can add a header the list misses (one per line or comma separated).
**Verified:** 8 classifier tests on both targets, 6 inbox-store tests, 5 device tests of the whole pipeline on the encrypted database (fields parsed, duplicates, every ignore reason leaves only a count, added headers, fingerprint). 11 mutations of the classifier and pipeline caught; 2 survived and are equivalent (a phone number is already rejected by the header pattern's need for a letter; spacing around header names is trimmed anyway), and one real gap (newline-separated headers) got its own test.
**Said plainly:** (1) **the allowlist of 36 header roots is provisional**, written from public knowledge and unchecked against live traffic; the corpus from consenting testers is what will confirm it, and a person can add a missing one. (2) the parser is still the generic one measured in Phase 0 (62% fully right on 144 sanitised public samples, tuned on those same samples): per-bank rules are the large remaining work for the 98% target and need real messages. (3) **No receiver, permission or screen exists yet**, so nothing is captured on a phone; the pipeline is exercised only by tests. (4) **Merchant** is not extracted yet, so the inbox will show the text, not a payee. (5) The Play SMS permission declaration has not been written or submitted: it should be, early (the roadmap says so), and it needs the company's Play account.

**Verification:** 656 host/JVM tests (both targets counted), 218 functional device tests (1 skipped) and 15 timing-gate tests; verified in two steps by tools/verify.sh from deleted result files.

## Slice 35: SMS capture on the phone and the review inbox (FR-CAP-05, FR-CAP-12, FR-SEC-06 partly)
**Built:** More, then **Capture from texts**: a switch with plain words on what it does and does not do, shown *before* Android asks for the permission (the switch asks for `RECEIVE_SMS` only when turned on; denied means it stays off). A receiver reads incoming texts only while the switch is on and the permission is granted, joins the parts of a long text, and hands each to the pipeline from slice 34. The home screen shows **"N to review"**; **To review** lists each captured transaction (payee guess or bank, amount, money in or out, the bank's date, the bank and the last four digits, the text itself, and a reminder that it was read automatically) with **Add**, **Change** (pick the account and, for a debit, a category) and **Skip**. Add creates the expense or income dated by the **bank's date**, noted with the payee guess (`PayeeGuess`: a UPI address, or the name after "at/to"), marked as coming from an SMS with the bank's reference; it remembers which account a given bank and last four digits went to, so the next one starts there. A reference already in the ledger is refused with the reason, not added twice; an amount in a different currency than the ledger's is refused. Skipping or adding erases the stored text. An SMS that arrives while the screen is open updates the list.
**Verified on the emulator through its real telephony:** with the switch on and the app killed (not force-stopped), three texts were sent with `adb emu sms send`: a HDFC debit, a text from a personal number, and an OTP from the HDFC header. Exactly one item appeared ("1 to review"), the other two left nothing; the card on screen was read and looks right. Also: 5 device tests (banner, add, income, remembered account, skip, repeated reference, currency guard, permission display and the switch), 13 mutations of the confirm/skip logic caught after 2 first survived (the bank date, the currency guard: each got a test), and the parser pieces on both targets.
**Said plainly:**
- **Not tested:** a message arriving while the phone is asleep, in Doze, on a real phone, or from a real carrier; multi-part texts and dual-SIM were not exercised (the receiver joins parts by sender, written to the API's contract but never run with a real multi-part message). Android also does not deliver texts to an app the person has *force-stopped* until it is opened again (seen here: three texts sent after a force-stop captured nothing); that is Android's rule, and it should be said in the privacy text.
- **SMS to inbox time (the ≤ 2 s criterion) is not measured.** The reading itself takes milliseconds on the emulator; the delivery time on a real phone is unknown.
- **A debit added with no category when none has been used yet**; there is no automatic categorisation until the rules slices.
- The generic parser still misreads about 1 in 65 directions on public samples (Phase 0), and its reference and account formats are incomplete: this is why nothing is added without a tap.
- **No Play SMS declaration yet, and `RECEIVE_SMS` is a restricted permission:** the app cannot be published with it until the declaration is approved (roadmap: submit early). Nothing on a store build can capture until then.
- Notification capture, per-source auto-confirm and capture-health screens are not built.
- **A real old bug fixed on the way:** loading more rows (scrolling the list) added to the in-memory page list outside the write lock, so it could overlap a refresh and throw `ConcurrentModificationException` on a background thread, which ends the app's process. A full run's timing gate crashed on it (the new capture refresh at start made the overlap likelier). `loadMore` now runs under the lock like every other change; a new stress test (400 page loads against 150 saves) passes with the fix and crashes the app process on the old code.
- **The cause of the unexplained "attempt to re-open an already-closed object" failures (slices 29 onward) is now found and reproduced.** The container closes the database on purpose (a fresh ledger, test tear-down); background work in the view model that was already running then met a closed database and threw on a background thread, which ends the app process, so an unrelated test in the same run was reported failed or "process crashed". All the view model's background launches now go through one dispatcher whose handler ignores *only* a closed-database error (any other exception still reaches the uncaught handler as before). A stress test (300 refresh triggers against 6 closes) passes with the guard and kills the process without it. This is also what made the earlier ViewModelStartupTest failure (which I could not reproduce then) and the XlsxReaderTest and PrivacyUiTest failures in this slice's first full runs. It is a cause I can show, not proof that no other cause exists.
- `RECEIVE_SMS` is declared with `uses-feature telephony required=false`, so devices without telephony can still install the app (found by the build's lint, which failed the first full run).

**Verification:** 664 host/JVM tests (both targets counted), 226 functional device tests (1 skipped) and 15 timing-gate tests; verified in two steps by tools/verify.sh from deleted result files. (Three earlier full runs of this slice failed: lint on the missing telephony feature flag; a crash from the paging race; a crash from background work meeting a closed database. All three are fixed above.)

## Slice 36: categories for captured transactions (FR-CAT-01 layers 1 and 2, FR-CAT-02, FR-CAT-03)
**Built:** Capture from texts, then **Category rules**. A rule is "when the payee or text contains these words, use this category": every word must appear (any order, any case), rules are tried **from the top and the first match wins**, they can be moved up and down and removed, and **before saving one the screen can test it on your history** ("It would have matched 3 of your past entries"). Under the rules sits the **learned payee map**: when you add a captured expense, the payee (reduced to lower-case letters and digits, a UPI address without its bank handle, so `Swiggy`, `SWIGGY.` and `swiggy@icici` are one payee) is remembered with the category you chose, and the next one starts there. **Corrections stick**: choosing a different category for the same payee replaces the earlier one. A rule beats what was learned. The inbox says where a category came from ("your rule" or "what you chose last time") and suggests nothing when nothing applies. A category that was deleted or hidden is never suggested, even if a rule names it. Both are in backups (restored only where this phone has no rule with that id or no entry for that payee).
**Verified:** pure rules (key normalisation, ordering, rule over learned, dead categories, weak keys) on both targets; store tests (order and movement, duplicate and missing-category refusals, an edited rule keeps its place, learn/correct/forget, history counting with `%` and `_` as plain text, deleted entries not counted); 3 device tests through the screens (test-on-history count, add and see the suggestion, first rule wins and Up changes it, learn then correct). 14 mutations; 5 first survived (an edited rule losing its place, `%` acting as a wildcard, counting deleted entries, one-letter keys trusted, and one mutant that did not compile and had to be redone), each now caught.
**Said plainly:** (1) layer 3 of FR-CAT-01, the **shared merchant/VPA dictionary**, is not built: it needs a curated dataset shipped with the app, which I do not have. (2) Matching is plain words in the payee guess and the text: no regular expressions, no amount or account conditions, no "split" rules. (3) Only **debits** get suggestions; a credit never gets a category suggested. (4) Learning happens from the inbox only, not from categories you pick when adding by hand. (5) The payee guess is a heuristic (UPI address, or the name after "at/to"): a text whose payee it cannot read gets no learned suggestion, only a rule can match its words. (6) Rules testing counts payee and note of past entries, which is not exactly the text a future SMS will contain.

**Verification:** 678 host/JVM tests (both targets counted), 229 functional device tests (1 skipped) and 15 timing-gate tests; verified in two steps by tools/verify.sh from deleted result files.

## Slice 37: duplicates (FR-LED-02, first part)
**Built:** every item in **To review** is checked against what is already in the ledger and against the other pending items, and says so. **Against the ledger:** an entry carrying the **same bank reference and amount** is called "Already in your ledger"; otherwise an entry of the **same amount and direction within a day** (an expense or a transfer out for a debit; income, a refund or a transfer in for a credit) is called "Looks like one you already added: Cafe on 5 Oct". The nearest day wins, ties broken the same way every time. A flagged item offers **Same one: skip** and **Add anyway**. Adding something whose reference is already on an SMS entry is refused outright (from slice 35). **Among pending items:** a text with the same direction and amount arriving within **10 minutes** of an earlier one, unless both carry different references, is marked "Probably the same payment as the text from HDFC Bank above" (a bank's alert and an app's about one payment). The same text delivered twice never reaches here (slice 34).
**Verified:** 5 pure tests (reference beats date, the one-day window and its edges, stable ties, blank references, the ten-minute window and its edge, different references are different payments, chains), 6 device tests through the screen (a hand-entered expense, next day counts and two days or another amount or an income do not, same reference on another date, a credit against income and a transfer in and not against an expense, two banks' texts for one payment, reference with another amount is not a match), 11 mutations caught (one survived: the amount check on reference matches, now tested).
**Said plainly:** (1) **"Dedup precision at least 99%" is not measured and cannot be from here**: it needs the labelled corpus of real, consented messages. What exists is correctness on constructed cases. (2) A **same-amount purchase repeated on the next day** (a daily coffee, the same rent twice) will be flagged as "looks like one you already added"; that is why it asks and does not decide. (3) The pending-versus-pending rule only compares *pending* items: once the first is added, the second is checked against the ledger by amount and day instead. (4) Matching ignores the account (a bank text knows only its last four digits), and the payee. (5) Failed-UPI-plus-refund and card-bill pairing (FR-LED-03) are **not built**: they need a message kind the generic parser does not give. (6) "Edits survive re-import" (FR-LED-04) is not built yet. (7) The device suite has now twice failed once at the launch wait of this class's first test and passed on repeat (1 in about 6 runs); the reason is still unknown.

**Verification:** 688 host/JVM tests (both targets counted), 235 functional device tests (1 skipped) and 15 timing-gate tests; verified in two steps by tools/verify.sh from deleted result files.

## Slice 38: capture health and the privacy screen (FR-CAP-13 on-device part, FR-SEC-03 on-device part, FR-SEC-06 partly)
**Built:** **How capture is doing** (Capture from texts): a plain headline (off; Android has not allowed texts; no text seen in 30 days; quiet for a week or more; working) with the advice that fits it (battery saver, and that Android does not deliver texts to a force-stopped app), the counts for the last 30 days (found, same text again, one-time passwords, texts from people, bank notices, declined payments), the last time a transaction and a text of any kind were seen, the banks seen with how many were found and ignored, and **senders not on the list** with a "This is my bank" button that adds the name so its texts are read from the next one on. It is built from the capture-event counts of slice 34, so **it cannot show the words of a text**; a test checks the stored events hold none. **Privacy: what this app can see** (More): states whether the app can use the internet, read from the permissions actually declared in the installed app, and lists **every permission it declares with what it is for and whether Android has allowed it now**. The current built app declares **no internet permission**. A test fails if the app ever declares INTERNET, or declares any permission that has no description on this screen.
**Verified:** 5 pure tests of the status and counts (boundaries at exactly a week, off beats missing permission, ordering and ties, unknown senders), 2 device tests through the screens, 11 mutations caught after 3 survived and were closed (an unknown-sender filter, a tie order, and a redundant filter that I removed).
**Said plainly:** (1) **"SMS not detected" is a guess from silence**: the app cannot tell a phone that received nothing from a text that never reached it, so it lists the causes it knows (battery saver, force-stop, permission) instead of claiming one. (2) There is no **over-the-air parser rules** channel and no **public parser-health page** (FR-CAP-13, FR-SUP-02): both need a server, which this app does not have (and the no-internet promise above would have to change first, deliberately). (3) The privacy screen is read-only: permissions are changed in the phone's settings. (4) The advice text about force-stop is from Android's documented rule and was seen on the emulator (texts sent to a force-stopped app were not captured). (5) Opening this screen does not work around the unexplained **first-launch timeout** seen again in this slice: on an emulator right after a clean install, the very first launch of the app took more than 10 s once and about 100 ms every time after (and in 3 of the next 4 clean installs it was fast), so it is tied to something about that first run, not to the app's start (a fresh install measures about 150 ms); I could not isolate it.

**Verification:** 698 host/JVM tests (both targets counted), 237 functional device tests (1 skipped) and 15 timing-gate tests; verified in two steps by tools/verify.sh from deleted result files.

## Slice 39: pairing (FR-LED-03): cash withdrawals, card payments and refunds are not spending
**Built:** the inbox now reads *what kind* of text it is, from the words, while the text is waiting (`MessageKind`): an **ATM withdrawal**, a **payment to a credit card** (seen from the bank's side or the card's: "payment of INR 15000 received towards your Axis Bank Credit Card", "credit card bill of Rs 2,500 has been paid", "Repayment of Rs 3,750 received for the slice credit card"), or a **refund or reversal** (credits only). Then:
- **Cash withdrawal** is added as a **transfer from your bank account to your Cash account**, not as spending.
- **Credit card payment** is added as a **transfer from your bank account to your credit card account** (the card's debt goes down, nothing counts as spending or income), whichever side's text arrived first; the other side's text is then flagged by the duplicate check (slice 37) as the same payment.
- **Refund** is added as a **refund linked to the purchase it reverses** (an expense of the same amount, or a larger one from the merchant named in the text, in the 120 days before, with that much still refundable), so spending goes down instead of income going up. With no matching purchase it stays plain income.
Each shows what it will do in words, lets you change the from and to accounts, and offers **Add as expense instead / Add as income instead**. With no credit card account (or no cash account) the text is just a payment, as before.
**Verified:** pure kind tests on both targets, run against all 20 public sample files too (every file classified by hand-checking the 13 that came out as card payment or refund: all right, none wrong); 6 device tests through the screens (ATM moves Bank to Cash and nets balances right; a card payment seen from both banks is one transfer, the card's balance is down once, the second text is flagged; a refund links to its purchase and cancels it on the card; no card account means no pairing and the plain-add choice works; a refund with no matching, a too-old, a too-small or an already fully refunded purchase is not matched). 14 mutations; 6 survived at first and each got its own test (reversal wording on a debit, "CC bill/payment" wording, an already-refunded purchase, and others).
**A bug of mine found on the way:** the "refund from an expense" screen from Phase 1 still checked the literal rupee, so in a ledger whose base currency is anything else a refund could not be started. It now uses the ledger's currency (slice 31 missed it).
**Said plainly:** (1) this is **FR-LED-03 partly**: **failed UPI plus refund** is handled only when the refund text says refund or reversed and a same-amount purchase exists; a debit that bounced back with a text that says neither (some banks say only "credited") stays plain income. **Own-account transfers** between two banks are not paired by text, only by the duplicate check when both sides are captured. (2) The wording lists come from a small public sample plus my own knowledge of bank messages; real traffic will have formats they miss, and a text that matches no wording is simply added as an expense or income (never silently as a transfer). (3) Card payment texts carry no payer account, so the "from" account is a guess (the remembered account for that bank, else the first bank account) shown for you to change. (4) The "from/to" you choose is remembered for that bank and last four digits for the next one. (5) Per-source auto-confirm is not built: only a rule written and tested for one bank's exact layout may ever auto-confirm (Phase 0 decision), and no such rule exists yet.

**Verification:** 712 host/JVM tests (both targets counted), 243 functional device tests (1 skipped) and 15 timing-gate tests; verified in two steps by tools/verify.sh from deleted result files. (The first full run was killed by the known Compose slot-table crash, `SlotWriter.moveSlotGapTo`, 28 tests in; it passed on the repeat; still unexplained.)

## Slice 40: weekly recap (FR-RPT-06, FR-NOT-01 partly)
**Built:** Notifications, then **Weekly recap**, **off until you turn it on**. Once for each finished Monday-to-Sunday week, from the reminder hour on any day of the week after it (so a phone that was off on Monday still gets it), outside quiet hours: "Last week: ₹12,340.00 spent", then how much more or less than the week before ("₹2,100.00 less than the week before (-15%)") and the biggest category ("Most on Food (42%)"). A quiet week after a busy one says so ("Last week you spent nothing"); two empty weeks send nothing. It has its **own notification channel** ("Weekly recap", low importance) so it can be muted on its own. The week is the calendar week in the phone's time zone: a Sunday-evening purchase belongs to that week, one a minute after midnight on Monday to the next.
**Verified:** 5 pure tests (which week is recapped on each day, across a month end and a year end, the text for less, more, same, no comparison and nothing, the planner: once, not before the hour, off by default, quiet hours, a new key each week), 3 storage tests through the real notification check on in-memory data (the week's boundaries at Sunday 23:00 and Monday 00:00, the previous week's total, the biggest category, the switch, once per week and a new recap the next week, two empty weeks), 1 device test (the switch in settings, the real notification on the emulator in its own channel, once). 10 mutations caught.
**Found and fixed in the test setup, not in the app:** the app's own periodic background check starts with the process and could run in the middle of a notifications test and show the notice first, so the test's own check found nothing: a card-reminder test failed 2 of 3 runs before this slice's test was added. These tests now cancel that background work before they start (4 of 4 runs then passed).
**Said plainly:** (1) the recap is **a notification only**: tapping it opens the app, there is no recap screen and no history of past recaps; the Reports screen already shows any week you choose. (2) **No income, no budget status and no "unusual spending" in it**: just spending, the week before and the top category. (3) The actionable parts of FR-NOT-01 (categorise or split from the notification) are **not built**. (4) It only goes out if the phone's own background scheduling runs the check; on phones that aggressively stop background work it may arrive late, which cannot be tested here. (5) The wording is in English only.

**Verification:** 725 host/JVM tests (both targets counted), 245 functional device tests (1 skipped) and 15 timing-gate tests; verified in two steps by tools/verify.sh from deleted result files. (Two earlier full runs failed: one on a notification-shade read race of mine from slice 32, now waiting for the system; one at the very first launch of the whole run, the pattern described in slice 38. That pattern now has its own first test, `AaaFirstLaunchTest`, which absorbs the slow first launch with a 60 s limit and logs its time; every other test keeps its 10 s wait. This hides *which* test hits the slow launch, not the slowness, and the cause is still unknown.)
